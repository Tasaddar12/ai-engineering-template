# Recover an isolated run without losing its boundary

A rejected, blocked, halted or over-scoped isolated run remains isolated through recovery. Preserve its exact path, branch, base, commits, dirty changes, failure evidence and handoff. Confirm worker stop before another worker touches that ownership. The authorized recovery is a fresh narrowly scoped isolated checkout or inspection of the preserved failed checkout. Do not propose editing the primary checkout as a default recovery path. A human may decide an actual scope/acceptance change; routine technical recovery stays within the existing authorization.

## Cleanup needs merge evidence

A commit existing on the worker branch does not show that integration succeeded. Require its current HEAD to be an ancestor of the actual integrated session HEAD, together with exact ownership and recorded lifecycle evidence, before cleanup. A changed HEAD, dirty tree, unknown owner or missing ancestry preserves the worktree and branch. Route deletion through the guarded runtime cleanup path; broad filesystem removal or branch deletion is not a substitute. Worktrees do not isolate shared databases, ports, accounts, caches, ignored data, remote resources or external services. Before cleanup or retry, identify which of those resources the worker used, preserve any needed evidence or data outside the checkout, and coordinate ownership and recovery separately. Branch ancestry alone cannot authorize deleting shared data, caches, credentials, accounts or external resources. A blocked worker's resources remain preserved until a concrete authorized disposition exists.
